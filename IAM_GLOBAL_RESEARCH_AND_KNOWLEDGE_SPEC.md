## 1. Scope

IAM's research scope covers publicly available human knowledge and institutions: people, organisations, institutions, industries, technologies, societies, events and the wider world.

This document **closes the research scope**. Additional domains should not be added indefinitely; remaining work belongs in gap analysis, implementation planning and validation.

Two permanent distinctions govern everything here:

- **Publicly accessible does not mean unrestrictedly usable.** Access conditions, licences, privacy laws and terms apply per source and jurisdiction.
- **Learning about something is not accepting it as true.** Knowledge must be evidence-based, dated, traceable and correctable.

## 2. Human Public Presence Intelligence (HPPI / PPIICE)

Research and maintain accurate, source-backed knowledge about people using **permitted public information** — the publicly presented identity, not the private identity.

### 2.1 What IAM may learn
- Publicly stated name, role, biography, stated interests.
- Professional presence: company, projects, career history, achievements, published work.
- Online presence: personal sites, blogs, portfolios, public profiles, community contributions.
- Publicly documented affiliations, collaborations and organisations.
- Interests and expertise demonstrated in public material.
- Public activity over time: role changes, projects, published positions, announcements.
- Authenticity indicators with recorded uncertainty rather than assumed matches.

### 2.2 How IAM researches
Using UWAIE and the Autonomous Research and Learning Engine: find public pages → read publicly accessible material → compare across sources → build a structured profile with source links and dates → flag contradictions, stale data and uncertain matches → update when new public information appears.

### 2.3 Boundaries
No private accounts, leaked databases, stolen data or unauthorised access; no bypassing logins, CAPTCHAs, paywalls or restrictions; no de-anonymisation; no sensitive-characteristic inference (religion, health, sexuality, political belief); a public profile is never proof of real-world identity; no impersonation, harassment, stalking or manipulation; separate from private visitor memory.

### 2.4 Modules
Human Public Presence Intelligence Engine · Public Identity Verification Engine · Public Profile Discovery Engine · Public Activity Research Engine · Public Information Provenance Engine · Human Knowledge Graph · Public Information Update Engine · Privacy and Ethical Research Guard.

## 3. Public Records Intelligence System

Discover, access, interpret, verify and learn from legally accessible public records worldwide. Distinct from public-presence research because records are official artefacts with their own authority and access rules.

### 3.1 Final consolidated scope
- **Civil registration:** birth, adoption where accessible, marriage, divorce, death.
- **Estates:** probate, executors, inheritance, succession, trusts, unclaimed inheritances.
- **Property:** deeds, land, transfers, historical ownership, registered interests.
- **Legal:** judgments, court notices, insolvency, liquidation, sequestration.
- **Business:** registrations, directors where disclosed, ownership disclosures, filings, status.
- **Government:** legislation, gazettes, official notices, public statistics.
- **Security:** publicly released police, military and security information.
- **Financial:** published disclosures, estate duty, unclaimed assets.
- **Professional:** licences, registrations, suspensions, disciplinary decisions.
- **Historical:** archives, census records, historical civil registers.
- **Regulatory:** enforcement notices, sanctions, compliance publications.
- **Procurement:** tenders, awards, supplier restrictions, public contracts.

### 3.2 Vital records, death and estates
Research death registrations/notices, obituaries, historical death records, burial registers, official inquests; deceased-estate registration, executors, letters of executorship, probate, wills where accessible, intestate succession, beneficiary rights, inventories, notices, creditor claims, distributions, unclaimed assets; related insolvency, trust and property transfers. Full civil lifecycle: birth → adoption → marriage → divorce → death → succession → estates.

South African initial sources: Department of Home Affairs, Master of the High Court, SARS, Government Printing Works, Deeds Office, SAFLII. Equivalent systems must be learned per country.

### 3.3 Capabilities
Search official databases; retrieve public documents; OCR scanned material; extract names, dates, organisations, relationships, events; cross-reference registries; detect inconsistencies and potentially fraudulent documents; track changes; build source-backed timelines; explain uncertainty; preserve originals, links, dates and verification history; share approved general knowledge with trusted nodes.

### 3.4 Safeguards
Respect access conditions and privacy law; never treat an allegation, arrest or filing as proof of wrongdoing; distinguish allegations, charges, judgments, convictions and overturned decisions; respect sealed/expunged/protected records; stricter controls for minors; no harassment, discrimination, identity theft or invasive profiling; correction and deletion procedures where required; verify death before treating someone as deceased; protect identity numbers, addresses, beneficiary and family details. Public availability never implies free, downloadable, searchable or legally reusable.

## 4. Public Security and Defence Intelligence Research

Study publicly available information about security organisations as research and knowledge — **not** as operational military, police or intelligence capability.

### 4.1 Coverage
Military and armed forces; police services; intelligence services' publicly acknowledged mandates; border/immigration; national security agencies; cybersecurity agencies; emergency services; prisons and correctional services; coast guards; international security organisations.

Learn structure, public responsibilities and legal powers, history, publicly disclosed equipment categories and technologies, training/doctrine/standards, security legislation, counterterrorism policy and public threat assessments, critical infrastructure protection, disaster response, international cooperation and peacekeeping, human rights and oversight frameworks.

### 4.2 Initial sources
SAPS · SANDF · State Security Agency · Department of Defence (ZA) · INTERPOL · UNODC · NATO · Europol · CISA · FBI · United Nations.

### 4.3 Boundaries
No classified, restricted or leaked information; no impersonation of personnel; no unauthorised access to government/security systems; no surveillance, no identification of operational vulnerabilities for exploitation; no facilitation of attacks; follow IAM Aegis white-hat restrictions for defensive research.

## 6. Cross-cutting research capabilities

- Multilingual global research, translation and regional terminology.
- Historical context and development of institutions/events.
- Real-time awareness of permitted public announcements and developments.
- Source reliability: official records vs independent reporting vs opinion vs rumour/misinformation.
- Conflicting information retained as uncertainty, not forced into one conclusion.
- Knowledge freshness with periodic rechecking of important facts.
- Cross-domain reasoning without confusing correlation with causation.
- Research memory: findings, original sources, summaries and verification history without duplicate bloat.
- Multimodal research: text, images, diagrams, audio, video, documents.
- Scientific reasoning: hypotheses, methodology, evidence, reproducibility, limitations.
- Relationship discovery between people, companies, technologies, events and institutions using verified evidence.
- Trend detection and evidence-based forecasting with explicit uncertainty.
- Research collaboration: compare findings across trusted nodes and independent models.
- Knowledge correction: update or retract without silently rewriting historical evidence.
- Research prioritisation by relevance, usefulness, risk and available resources.
- Knowledge portability independent of model, hosting or storage provider.
- Research reproducibility: preserve source material, methods and versions.

---

## 7. Learning versus collecting

IAM must not try to download and store the internet. It discovers broadly, researches selectively, verifies before accepting, preserves important original evidence, compresses/deduplicates, retains provenance, respects copyright/privacy/licences/access, and revisits stale information. This keeps knowledge useful within the resource budget (including the 1% node contribution policy).

## 9. Acceptance criteria

1. Research uses only genuinely public, permitted information; access controls are never bypassed.
2. Every stored claim has provenance, dates and a confidence/uncertainty marker.
3. Allegations, opinions and verified facts are clearly distinguished.
4. Corrections preserve history rather than silently overwriting evidence.
5. Sensitive personal characteristics and private visitor data are never compiled or exposed.
6. Public presence, records and security research remain distinct capabilities with distinct controls.
7. Knowledge remains portable and syncs to trusted nodes only with approval.
8. Research activity respects the 1% contribution ceiling and avoids resource exhaustion.
